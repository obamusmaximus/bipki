# Combat (enemy lock, strike, melee, throw, bullet time)

Read this before working on combat. Update it at the end of the task (see `docs/mechanics/README.md`). Name tunables, never their values.

**Scripts:** `enemy/enemy.gd` (`Enemy` base: states, thrown physics, knockback, impact damage), `player/strike_guidance.gd`, `melee.gd`, `enemy_throw.gd`, `throw_preview.gd`, `bullet_time.gd`, `head_look.gd` (camera weight), `health.gd`, `hud/throw_readout.gd`, `hud/damage_numbers.gd`, `enemy/enemy_effects.gd`. Tunables: `GrappleStats` ("Enemy hook", "Launch at an enemy", "Throw"), `PlayerStats` (melee), `EnemyStats` (health, thrown flight, impact, knockback, stun), `HudStats` ("Damage numbers").
**Related:** the grapple itself is in `grapple.md`. Drone AI is in `drones.md`.

## Intent
There is no gun. Enemies are thrown, punched at speed, or used as anchors, so combat is movement. A hit should feel weighty: a ka-ching, slow-mo, knockback.

## How it works
- **Enemy:** the base class (`Enemy`, a `CharacterBody3D` on layer 3, in group `enemies`) for everything hookable. Its states are NORMAL (its own behaviour), GRABBED (held by a throw), THROWN (flying free) and STUNNED (survived a throw, falls, can be hooked again, recovers after `stun_time`). It owns a `Health` node, dies and frees itself. `Drone` is the first. A new enemy type extends `Enemy` with its own stats class extending `EnemyStats`; a variant is just another `.tres`.
- **Enemy lock (snap):** a fire locks onto an enemy the crosshair ray hits, or the one nearest the crosshair within `enemy_snap_degrees` (plus its apparent size) with a clear line to it. The hit point follows the enemy and the tip homes in on it.
- **Launch at an enemy (strike):** an RMB tap (released before `throw_hold_time`) on an enemy launches the player into it and arms the melee hit. `StrikeGuidance` keeps the speed a normal launch would give (never faster) but solves the direction: an arc under the launch gravity (kept on for the whole flight) into where the enemy will be (`strike_lead`). In flight it turns the velocity back onto that arc at up to `strike_turn_rate_degrees`. Air steering and the soft speed limit still apply; the obstacle assist is off during it. Steering more than `strike_veer_degrees` away from the enemy, passing it, landing, a hit or `strike_max_time` ends the guidance.
- **Melee (body hit):** `Melee`. Flying into an enemy at `melee_min_speed` or more, or at any speed while armed by a launch at it (armed until landing or `melee_armed_time`), punches it. The hit sphere sits in front of the player along the look direction (`melee_reach`, `melee_hit_radius` plus the enemy's radius, within `melee_front_degrees`), along the flight while armed, and is swept between frames. A hit plays a synthesized metallic ka-ching (`Synth.make_clang`), slows time for `melee_slowmo_time` real seconds (the throw's bullet time), deals `melee_damage_fraction` of the enemy's max health and knocks it back with the player's velocity. The player keeps `melee_momentum_keep` of their speed and gets the double jump back. A swing on that enemy is released. The player doesn't collide physically with enemies; `Melee` does its own overlap test.
- **Knockback:** `Enemy.knock_back` flies like a thrown enemy (a surface hit does impact damage and stuns), but after `knockback_max_distance` with no hit it regains control at once. While it flies it is still hookable (`Enemy.is_hookable` counts THROWN with the knockback flag), so the player can grapple it, throw it or strike it again before it regains control (`Melee` shares the check; `melee_cooldown` stops a double hit). A real throw is not hookable mid-flight, only once it is stunned. `grab()` clears the knockback flag.
- **Throw:** RMB held for `throw_hold_time` with the hook on an enemy (`EnemyThrow`, `Mode.THROW`). Bullet time starts and the enemy hangs where it is. Releasing RMB (or `throw_duration` real seconds passing) sends it flying. The player is not frozen: they carry on at bullet-time speed with their momentum.
- **Bullet time:** `BulletTime` scales `Engine.time_scale` to `bullet_time_scale` (eased), slows and muffles the audio, and kicks the FOV. Godot keeps ticking physics at the normal rate and scales the step, so it is smooth. Anything that times itself in real seconds divides its physics delta by `Engine.time_scale`. Mouse look and the HUD run in real time.
- **Pull point / throw direction:** the enemy flies from where it hangs toward the pull point, `throw_reach` ahead of the camera along the look direction (and `throw_hand_offset` to the right). A rope only pulls toward the hand, so a far enemy can only be sent back past the player. The player positions themselves on the line between the enemy and the target. A large `throw_reach` makes it more arcade (it goes about where you look). A throw bends up to `throw_assist_degrees` toward another enemy near its path.
- **Draw and flick (throw power):** draw is how far the player has turned away from the enemy (0 facing it, 1 directly away). Flick is the camera's fastest turn within `throw_flick_window` before release. `throw_draw_weight` and `throw_flick_weight` mix them into a power from 0 to 1, which sets the speed between `throw_min_speed` and `throw_max_speed`. Releasing while facing the enemy (within `throw_min_draw_degrees`) is a limp release: the enemy is just dropped.
- **Camera weight:** mouse sensitivity falls toward `throw_weight_min_sensitivity` as the draw grows (`HeadLook.sensitivity_scale`).
- **Impact damage:** a thrown enemy that hits a surface takes `damage_for_impact(speed)`: the part of its velocity into the surface (not its full speed, so a graze does little), minus `impact_threshold`, times `damage_per_speed`. It then bounces and is stunned. A thrown enemy that hits another enemy damages it too (`collision_damage_scale`).
- **Release prediction:** while a throw is being aimed, `ThrowPreview` sweeps a sphere against the world and other enemies. It feeds the orange line on `TrajectoryArc`, a marker at the first hit, and the damage (or KILL) it would do. `ThrowReadout` draws the power ring, the time ring and an arrow to the held enemy at the screen edge when it is out of view.
- **Damage numbers:** floating numbers on the HUD where an enemy takes damage (`DamageNumbers`, driven by `Enemy.damaged`). They rise and fade in real time, are bigger for harder hits, are red for the killing blow, and follow `hud_scale`.

## Settled (don't re-open without a reason from playtesting)
- **A strike is never faster than a normal launch.** Clicking an enemy must not unlock extra speed, so the soft speed limit stays on during it and the guidance only turns the velocity, never adds speed.
- **The player can always veer off a strike.** Air steering stays on during it; steering away ends the guidance instead of being fought.

## Tried and passed on
- **Fixed height offset for a launch at an enemy** (`launch_enemy_height_offset`): replaced by `StrikeGuidance`. It missed from above and from past about 20 m: the gravity drop grows with distance, carried momentum bent the line, and drones move. In a headless test in the arena (13 launches: level from 10 to 48 m, from above and below, straight down, falling fast, with sideways momentum, at moving drones), the old offset hit 8 of 13. The guidance hits 13 of 13, and also 13 of 13 with homing off.

## Known issues and parked
- Melee slow-mo and the throw's bullet time are separate `BulletTime` instances. Starting a throw inside the half-second melee slow-mo can make the two fight over `Engine.time_scale` for a moment.
- Killed enemies don't respawn in normal levels (only `EnemySpawner` respawns them; see `levels_and_hud.md`).
- The respawn model is tied to the rewind idea in `GAME_DESIGN.md`. Design enemies so their state can be captured.
